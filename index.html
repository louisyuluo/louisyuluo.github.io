Bio

Louis Yu Luo is a PhD candidate at Imperial Business School, supervised by Prof. Anu Wadhwa and Prof. Chengwei Liu. After more than a decade in energy, broadcasting and online education, he returned to academia to study organizations, innovation and the future of work.

His research focuses on contrarian strategies and the Theory-Based View (TBV), and on the impact of AI on individuals, organizations and society. He combines quantitative analysis with qualitative insight, drawing on machine learning, causal inference and field experiments.

Before his PhD he was an award-winning business correspondent and presenter, with exclusive interviews including Kevin Rudd, Dominic Barton, and Nobel laureates Thomas J. Sargent and Aaron Ciechanover. He has held senior roles in education companies, co-founded ventures including EZPZ Education in Beijing, and lectured in interpreting and translation.

He believes real innovation needs a contrarian perspective and human insight beyond existing data. Open to AI research collaborations; happy to start over a coffee.

Thesis thread

Contrarian strategy in an age of AI.

Strategy research usually treats differentiation as one thing: how far a firm sits from its peers. My dissertation separates it into two. A firm can be distant from others while still sharing their beliefs, or it can contradict the consensus about where value lies. Only the second is contrarian, and audiences treat the two very differently.

Core idea

Distance tells you how different a strategy looks. Contrarianism tells you whose beliefs it has to overturn to succeed.

I test this across venture funding, academic peer review and patents, and I study the formulation side with think-aloud protocols. Large language models run through the work twice: as a research instrument for measuring consensus at scale, and as a new kind of evaluator whose judgments may favour, or flatten, contrarian ideas.
